Selected Projects

Gas-Surface Interactions in Passive Intakes for Air Breathing Electric Propulsion
Justus Liebig University
March 2026
PDF

Understanding the gas-surface interactions at orbital velocities is crucial for designing intakes for air breathing electric propulsion systems. To support this, I performed beam characterization measurements using Faraday Probes and Retarding Potential Analyzers. For two experimental setups, I made critical contributions that advanced both the hardware capabilities and the subsequent data analysis.

Atmospheric Re-Entry Simulations
Justus Liebig University
November 2025
PDF

In this project I simulated atmospheric re-entry by numerically solving the governing equations of motion and approximated the heat flux with the Sutton-Graves correlation. This made it possible to quantify the impact of different trajectory design parameters on the total mechanical and thermal loads. Characteristic trajectories were found for ballistic and aerodynamic re-entry.

Continuum Kinetic Plasma Simulations
University of Washington
June 2025
PDF

Numerical solutions of Vlasov's equation are developed on a 1D1V grid using Strang splitting and upwind methods. The method is applied to study Landau damping and the two-stream instability. While the simulations reproduce the expected physical processes, low numerical accuracy leads to deviations from theoretical growth rates.

Nonlinear MHD Plasma Dynamics, Toroidal Equilibria, Linear Stability
University of Washington
May 2025
PDF

Three different numerical approaches are applied to investigate plasma confinement devices. A 3D MHD simulation is used to analyze sausage and kink instabilities in a z-pinch. The Grad-Shafranov equation is solved numerically for toroidal equilibria and compared with an analytical solution, showing excellent agreement. Finally, a linear stability analysis of a spheromak's tilt mode is performed.

Accuracy, Stability, Convergence for Linear Advection and Ideal MHD Plasma Model
University of Washington
April 2025
PDF

This project explores five different numerical schemes that are used to analyze the linear advection equation. Through truncation-error and Von Neumann stability analysis, these algorithms are examined for their characteristics and compared to each other. Finally, one of the schemes is generalized to simulate the 1D ideal-MHD model and solve the Brio-Wu shock problem. The results from the corresponding paper are successfully reproduced.

Augmented Apollo Powered Descent Guidance Paper Discussion
University of Washington
March 2025
PDF

In this project, I analyzed and reproduced a novel powered descent guidance system for lunar and martian landings. I recreated key elements of the guidance system and visualized its performance, providing an in-depth analysis of its potential advantages and limitations. This work deepened my understanding of spacecraft control systems and their application in precision landings for deep space exploration.

Ion Optics Simulation of a GIE Extraction Grid
Justus Liebig University
July 2024
PDF

Gridded ion engines (GIE) use an extraction grid to accelerate ions to the desired velocity. I performed simulations of ion optics using IBSimu, a Linux-based framework, to determine optimal grid distances. By comparing different figures of merit, I identified the optimal configuration for maximizing thrust efficiency.

Spectroscopic Temperature Measurements to Characterize Solid-State Ion Sources
Justus Liebig University
June 2024
PDF

In my bachelor thesis, I developed and automated a temperature measurement setup using optical emission spectroscopy to characterize solid-state ion sources. The Richardson-Dushman equation relates temperature to emitted current density, and I applied this to the study of crystal emission properties. My results confirmed the expected behavior of the produced crystals and demonstrated the method's potential for optimizing their production.
